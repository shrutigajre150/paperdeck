## Q1: What is retrieval-augmented generation and how does it combine parametric and non-parametric memory?

**Answer:**

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a model that combines pre-trained parametric memory, represented by a seq2seq transformer like BART, with non-parametric memory, which is a dense vector index of Wikipedia accessed via a neural retriever. The model retrieves relevant documents based on the input and uses these documents as additional context for generating output. This approach allows RAG to leverage both the extensive knowledge stored in its parameters and the ability to access up-to-date information from external sources, enhancing performance on knowledge-intensive tasks [2005.11401, p.2; p.3]. The integration of these two memory types enables RAG to generate more factual and specific responses compared to models relying solely on parametric memory [2005.11401, p.1].

## Q2: How does a dense dual-encoder retriever compare to BM25 for open-domain question answering?

**Answer:**

The dense dual-encoder retriever, specifically the Dense Passage Retriever (DPR), significantly outperforms the BM25 method in open-domain question answering. In terms of top-5 accuracy, DPR achieves 65.2% compared to BM25's 42.9%, indicating a substantial improvement in retrieval performance. Additionally, DPR contributes to better end-to-end QA accuracy, with results of 41.5% versus 33.3% for BM25 [2004.04906, p.2]. Furthermore, DPR captures lexical variations and semantic relationships more effectively than BM25, which is sensitive to specific keywords [2004.04906, p.7]. ## Q4: What are intrinsic and extrinsic hallucinations in natural language generation?

**Answer:**

Intrinsic hallucinations in natural language generation (NLG) refer to outputs that contain incorrect information compared to the source content, such as a generated summary that contradicts the original text. For example, if the source states "The first vaccine for Ebola was approved by the FDA in 2019," a generated output saying "The first Ebola vaccine was approved in 2021" would be an intrinsic hallucination [2202.03629, p.4]. 

Extrinsic hallucinations, on the other hand, involve generated content that adds information not present in the source, making it impossible to verify against the original input. An example would be a generated response that includes details like "happily" or "with his friend" without any connection to the source material [2202.03629, p.30].

The reasoning traces help the model track and update action plans, while the actions enable it to gather information from external sources, thereby enhancing the overall task-solving capability and interpretability of the model's decisions [2210.03629, p.1][2210.03629, p.2].

## Q6: How can a language model teach itself to call external APIs such as a calculator or search engine?

**Answer:**

## Q8: How does a model decide when to retrieve and critique its own output using special reflection tokens?

**Answer:**

The model decides when to retrieve by generating a retrieval token based on the input prompt and preceding generations. If the model predicts that retrieval is necessary, it outputs a retrieval token that prompts a retriever model to fetch relevant passages. After retrieving, the model evaluates the relevance of these passages and generates critique tokens to assess the quality of its output based on the retrieved information. The process involves predicting various reflection tokens, including those that indicate the need for retrieval and those that critique the output's relevance and support [2310.11511, p.1; p.4; p.6].

## Q13: Why do models struggle to use documents placed in the middle of the prompt?

**Answer:**

## Q14: How can a model reduce factual errors by deciding on demand whether to retrieve?

**Answer:**

The model reduces factual errors by implementing a mechanism called Self-Reflective Retrieval-augmented Generation (SELF-RAG), which allows it to determine on demand whether retrieval of additional information is necessary for a given task. When presented with an input prompt, the model first assesses if augmenting its generation with retrieved passages would be beneficial. If deemed necessary, it outputs a retrieval token to call a retriever model, processes the retrieved passages, and generates task outputs based on their relevance. This approach aims to enhance factual accuracy while maintaining the model's versatility by ensuring that only relevant information is retrieved and utilized in the generation process [2310.11511, p.1; 2310.11511, p.11].

## Q15: What training or decoding choices lead to unfaithful generated text?

**Answer:**

Training and decoding choices that contribute to unfaithful generated text include imperfect representation learning, where an encoder fails to comprehend input text accurately, leading to erroneous generation. Additionally, decoders may attend to incorrect parts of the encoded input, resulting in mixed-up facts between similar entities. The design of the decoding strategy itself can also exacerbate hallucinations; for example, strategies that introduce randomness, like top-k sampling, are positively correlated with increased hallucination. Furthermore, exposure bias, which arises from discrepancies in decoding between training and inference times, is another significant contributor to unfaithful outputs [2202.03629, p.8; p.4].
